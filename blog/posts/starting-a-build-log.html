Starting a build log

Most of what I learn dies in a scratch file or scrolls off the top of a terminal. This is where I stop letting that happen.

I build and run things — a self-hosted homelab, the networks that hold it together, and the apps and platforms I ship on top. The work is fun. The problem is that almost none of it gets written down. A tricky fix gets solved at 1am, works, and is forgotten by the weekend. Six months later I hit the same wall and start from scratch.

So this is a build log. Not a polished tutorial site, not a personal brand exercise — a place to record the actual work while it's still fresh: what broke, what I tried, what turned out to be the real cause, and the small decisions that only make sense once you've lived with the consequences.

What I'll write about

Roughly the things I spend my days on:

  • Infrastructure & homelab — virtualization, networking, DNS, VLANs, and the self-hosting rabbit holes that come with running your own stack.
  • DevOps & platforms — CI/CD, reverse proxies, observability, and making systems boringly reliable.
  • Shipping software — the unglamorous last mile of getting an app from "works on my machine" to actually in front of users.
  • Debugging trails — full postmortems where the interesting part is the wrong turns, not just the answer.

How I'll write it

Three rules I'm holding myself to:

  1. Show the real path. The dead ends are usually more useful than the fix.
  2. Keep it reproducible. Commands, configs, and versions — enough that a future me (or you) could actually follow along.
  3. Stay honest. If something is a hack, I'll say it's a hack.
The best documentation I've ever written was the kind I wrote for myself, six months too late. This is me writing it on time.
